<Note>
  **TLDR:** A modern phone in landscape covers 95% of coaching footage. Recording 30+ minutes regularly? Add a tripod and an external mic. Skip a "real camera" unless slow-motion or low light is actually a problem.
</Note>


## The honest minimum

Start with the phone in your pocket. Any iPhone or Pixel from the last 4–5 years records 1080p at 30 fps that looks great on a laptop screen. Don't go shopping until you've felt the limits.

-   Phone in landscape, propped against a wall or on a tripod.
-   Camera at chest height of the action, not floor level — full bodies visible.
-   Even lighting; avoid backlighting (windows behind subjects).
-   Quiet space, or accept that ambient noise is part of the recording.

## Phones that work well

Anything that records 1080p H.264 at 30 fps with usable autofocus. Specifically:

-   **iPhone:** iPhone 11 forward. Default Camera app at 1080p30, or 1080p60 if you'll slow-mo in post. Switch **Settings → Camera → Formats** to "Most Compatible" rather than "High Efficiency" so the resulting file plays back in every browser.
-   **Pixel:** Pixel 4 forward. Default Camera at 1080p.
-   **Samsung Galaxy:** S20 forward. Set the codec to H.264 in Camera settings if it's offering HEVC.

## Tripods & mounts

The single highest-ROI accessory. A stable shot makes a 30-minute review watchable; handheld shake makes it unusable.

-   **Mini tripod / tabletop ($15–25)** — Joby GripTight ONE or a generic equivalent. Good for individual lessons in tight spaces.
-   **Full-height tripod ($30–60)** — Manfrotto Compact or any 60" travel tripod with a phone clamp. Easiest setup for a class recording.
-   **Wall mount** — phone clamp on a high shelf with a suction cup or screw mount. Great for academies recording every class from the same angle.

## Audio

Phone mics are fine inside 10 ft. Past that, voice gets muddy and ambient noise dominates.

-   **Lapel/lavalier mic ($20–50)** — Rode SmartLav+ or a generic. Plugs into the headphone port (USB-C with adapter for newer phones). Big jump in clarity for verbal instruction.
-   **Wireless mic ($60–150)** — Rode Wireless GO or DJI Mic. Lets the coach move around. Worth it for voiceover commentary.
-   **Skip:** shotgun mics on a phone are awkward and on-camera condensers rarely beat a phone in a noisy gym.

## Lighting

Most academies have plenty of overhead light. Trouble spots:

-   **Mixed light sources** — daylight from a window plus warm interior light shifts color across the frame. Close blinds or kill the interior lights.
-   **Strong backlight** — subject silhouetted against a bright window. Move the camera or cover the window.
-   **Dim corner** — a $20 LED panel or clip-on ring light fills it. Don't overspend.

## When to move past a phone

Only if you need one of these:

-   **Slow motion** at usable resolution (120–240 fps). GoPro Hero, Sony ZV-1, or any recent mirrorless.
-   **Low light** in a poorly lit gym. Larger sensor wins — mirrorless beats phone here.
-   **Wide shots from far back.** Action cams (GoPro) have wider field of view; useful for tournament footage.
-   **Long sessions (60+ minutes)** where phone overheating becomes a problem. A camera with active cooling outlasts a phone in summer.

## Settings that matter

-   **Resolution:** 1080p is enough; 4K is overkill and slower to upload.
-   **Frame rate:** 30 fps for normal review; 60 fps if you'll slow-mo in post.
-   **Codec:** H.264 ("Most Compatible" on iPhone). H.265/HEVC saves space but plays inconsistently across browsers.
-   **Stabilization:** on, if your phone offers it.
-   **Auto-focus tracking:** on for moving subjects. Lock focus only for stationary instruction footage.

## Storage on the phone

1080p H.264 is roughly 1 GB per 10 minutes. A 60-minute class is ~6 GB. Free up space before a long recording, or upload between classes so the camera roll doesn't fill.


<Tip>
  The only wrong rig is the one that's too complicated to set up before class. Start with what you have, add the tripod first, then the mic. Skip the camera unless slow-mo or low light is forcing your hand.
</Tip>
